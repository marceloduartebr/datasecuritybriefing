In one line: The October cyberattack already has a price on Ahold Delhaize’s balance sheet; what still has no public answer is what happened to the data.

## What happened

On 4 November 2026, when releasing third-quarter results, Ahold Delhaize (Food Lion, Hannaford, Albert Heijn) estimated that the October cyberattack, which took online operations offline in the United States and the Benelux, will reduce fourth-quarter sales by between €200 million and €250 million and operating profit by between €75 million and €100 million. The company cut its 2026 guidance. The statement does not inform the type of attack or whether personal data were affected.

## Why it matters

The financial impact of an incident is usually measured by the disruption. Regulatory exposure depends on another question: which personal data were in the affected systems and whose they were. In a group operating in several jurisdictions, that answer defines notification deadlines and recipients.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Disruption of online operations in the US and Benelux | Q3 release, 4 November | High |
| Financial impact and revision of 2026 guidance | Q3 release | High |
| Personal data of customers or employees | Not informed by the company | Indeterminate |

## What to do this week

1. Include in the crisis scenario the question “which personal data are in each critical system”, with a documented answer — owner: security and DPO.
2. Review with finance how the impact of an incident would be estimated and reported to investors — owner: CFO / investor relations.
3. Signal that it worked: for each critical system, the company can say which categories of data subjects and jurisdictions would be affected.
