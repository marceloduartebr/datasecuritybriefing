**In one line:** the government confirmed the access, but the one saying how much leaked is the one selling the data.

## What happened
On November 17, 2026, Bolivia's presidential spokesperson, José Luis Gálvez, acknowledged intrusion attempts against Executive branch institutions and said part of the information is being offered on the dark web. Senarecom confirmed unauthorized access to Sinacom, its mineral trading and export system. The Foreign Ministry identified a security incident, activated response and containment protocols, and is working with Agetic to determine whether data was exposed.

The Hacking Bolivia collective, which raised the alert on November 15, says it saw databases from four agencies for sale: Senarecom (654,414 records), the Foreign Ministry (more than 170 GB and 619,143 case files), the Army (more than 6 GB of documents) and the Senate (2,652 records). Authorities have not confirmed these figures or the attribution of the attack.

## Why it matters
The agencies named cover mineral exports, foreign relations, defense and the Legislature. Data of this kind fuels extortion, identity fraud and commercial espionage. And until the government publishes its own assessment, the only number available is the attacker's.

## Risk reading
| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized access to Executive systems | Confirmed by the government, Senarecom and the Foreign Ministry | High |
| Sale of databases on underground forums | Claimed by Hacking Bolivia; not officially verified | High |
| Scope per record and per data subject | Still under investigation (Foreign Ministry with Agetic) | To be confirmed |

## What to do this week
1. List which systems hold personal data and whose — owner: data protection officer (DPO).
2. Test whether the team can say, in under 72 hours, which records were in a breached system — owner: CISO.
3. Sign that it worked: the answer to "what got out?" comes from your inventory, not from a forum.
