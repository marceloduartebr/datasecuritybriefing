In one line: the structure companies point to when asked who is accountable for data protection shrank, while the data subject keeps handing data to chatbots and suffering scams.

## What happened

On Monday (31 Aug), Cetic.br / CGI.br released the third edition of the Privacy and Personal Data Protection in Brazil survey. Among firms with ten or more employees, the share that declared a named data-protection officer (DPO) fell from 19% in 2023 to 14% in 2025. A dedicated privacy area or person went from 25% to 20%. An incident-response plan, from 25% to 21%.

In the same survey, 32% of internet users aged 16 or over reported an attempted scam involving their own personal data; 20% said they had been victims. Almost a third already use an artificial-intelligence tool day to day, and 37% said they had given a name, address or CPF to a chatbot.

## Why it matters

A perception survey does not replace an inventory. The drop in internal-structure indicators — DPO, dedicated area, incident plan — happens in the same period in which the data subject hands data to language models and the scam arrives via the messaging app. The legal duty under the LGPD did not shrink. What shrank was the evidence that the company can point, clearly, to who is accountable and what actually exists in today’s org chart.

If ANPD asks for the officer and the incident plan tomorrow, the useful answer is not the 2023 document: it is the current map of who holds the role and which controls are actually running.
