In one line: the data left the vendor, but the duty to know who was affected remains with Baker Tilly.

## What happened
Baker Tilly Advisory Group, a US accounting and consulting firm (annual revenue of US$ 6.8 billion), is notifying clients and dependents about a breach discovered on 6 September 2026. An unauthorized party accessed data stored on a platform operated by an unnamed third-party service provider. Full name, address and Social Security number were exposed. The Texas Attorney General filing lists 10,456 people in that state alone; notices were also filed in California and South Carolina. The total has not been disclosed. The company states the incident occurred in the third-party environment, not on its network, engaged specialists, notified law enforcement and is offering 24 months of Experian monitoring. There is no report of misuse or ransomware claim. The report is from SC Media (12/11), based on BleepingComputer; the official notice was not re-read for this briefing.

## Why it matters
Accounting firms concentrate tax identifiers of clients and their dependents. When those data sit on a third-party platform, the incident happens outside the perimeter, but the duty to notify and to size the scope stays with the organization that collected the data.

## Risk reading
| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Third-party platform | Unauthorized access in the vendor environment | High |
| Tax identifier (SSN) | Name, address and SSN exposed, including dependents | High |
| Undefined scope | Total not disclosed; notifications by state | Medium |

## What to do this week
1. List the vendors that store client personal data and which fields each receives — owner: privacy/DPO.
2. Confirm in contract the incident-notice deadline and the right to obtain the list of affected records — owner: legal and procurement.
3. Signal that it worked: for any vendor, answer in hours how many data subjects and which fields are in its environment.
