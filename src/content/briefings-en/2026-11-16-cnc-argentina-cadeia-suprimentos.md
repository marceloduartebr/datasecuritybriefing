In one line: Argentina now requires manufacturer certification for public purchases of critical telecom; the missing proof is that of operation.

> Note: the Boletín Oficial text was not reread for this briefing; the summary follows the standard's record and the analysis published by CEADIGI.

## What happened

Argentina's National Cybersecurity Center (CNC) published in the Boletín Oficial on 6 November 2026 Disposition 7/2026, dated 5 November and signed by executive director Walter Fabián Agüero. The rule approves supply chain security requirements for critical telecommunications infrastructure equipment based on the TIA SCS 9001 standard, which provides for third-party certification of the manufacturer.

- It is mandatory for the national public sector, including state-owned companies, when purchasing, installing or operating such equipment.
- It is indicative for the private sector.
- The CNC has 90 days to define the infrastructures and categories of equipment reached and to publish a correlation matrix with the NIST CSF and ISO/IEC 27001.
- Article 9 asks Enacom to evaluate including the requirements in equipment homologation and in the obligations of ICT licensees.
- It takes effect the day after publication. The text does not name companies or exclude manufacturers by nationality.

## Why it matters

Certification answers where the equipment comes from and how it was built. It does not answer what data passes through it, who administers it and with what credential. For operators and state suppliers, the "indicative" rule tends to become a contract clause, and eventual adoption by Enacom would take the requirement to the entire licensed market.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment of unverified origin on critical network | Rule requires TIA SCS 9001 on public purchases | High |
| Third-party administrative access to equipment | Rule addresses the manufacturer, not operation | Medium |
| Scope still undefined | CNC has 90 days to define infrastructures and categories | Medium |

## What to do this week

1. Inventory telecom equipment in use under contracts with the Argentine public sector, with manufacturer and certifications — owner: procurement and network architecture.
2. Map who has administrative access to each piece of equipment, including third parties, and what data travels through it — owner: security and operations.
3. Signal it worked: when the CNC publishes the matrix with NIST CSF and ISO/IEC 27001, each control already has associated operational evidence, not just the purchasing policy.
