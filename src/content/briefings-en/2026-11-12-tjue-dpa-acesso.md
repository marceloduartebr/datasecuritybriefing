In one line: The CJEU closed the gap: the authority that enforces the right of access is also a controller and must answer it.

## What happened

On November 5, 2026, the Court of Justice of the European Union delivered the ruling in case C-205/25 (Lindenberg). The data protection authority, when processing data related to a complaint under Article 77 of the GDPR, acts as a controller. In principle, it must grant the complainant the access under Article 15. A generic exclusion by national law is incompatible. Access covers the complainant's personal data, not the entire administrative file; third-party interests may be protected by redaction or anonymization.

## Why it matters

Whoever demands evidence must produce their own. The case shows that the authority's processing is not outside the map. For companies, the same principle applies in the complaint process: what the authority saw and returned to the data subject needs inventory and response, not just internal policy.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|

| Confusion between administrative file and personal data | Access is to personal data, not the entire dossier | Medium |

## What to do this week

1. Review whether the privacy complaint process records what was shared with the authority and what was returned to the data subject — owner: privacy.
2. Confirm there is no generic exclusion of access in internal procedures — owner: legal.
3. Signal: access log and response to the data subject updated in the process.
