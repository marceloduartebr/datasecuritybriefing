In one line: the Foreign Ministry confirmed the security incident and, for now, has not confirmed whether there was unauthorized access or exposure of information.

## What happened

On 16 November 2026 the ANF news agency reported that Bolivia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said it had identified a computer security incident in its institutional systems and activated response and containment protocols. According to the statement cited by ANF and also by La Razón and El Deber, the ministry is working with the Agency for Electronic Government and Information and Communication Technologies (AGETIC) and carrying out technical analysis to determine the circumstances, establish whether there was unauthorized access or exposure of information and take further measures. The institution did not confirm theft, leakage or exposure of data and said it would avoid drawing conclusions about the causes without verified information, reporting progress through official channels. It also reaffirmed its commitment to protecting personal data and to keeping services to citizens running, in Bolivia and abroad.

## Why it matters

For a ministry that handles documents and services for citizens at home and abroad, confirming the incident without yet describing its reach leaves a gap: the notice exists, the map of what was read does not yet. That gap should not be filled with unverified figures circulating outside official channels.

## What to do this week

1. Handle alerts about public bodies in the region only with what the institution has confirmed, and log any unverified figure separately (owner: communications / DPO).
2. Review your own incident playbook: the first statement should say what is known and what is still under investigation, with a deadline for the data map (owner: incident response).
3. Inventory which document and public-service databases an equivalent incident would reach in your operation (owner: CISO / data protection officer).
