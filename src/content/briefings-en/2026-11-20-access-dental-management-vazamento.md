In one line: Access Dental Management confirmed access to its network between May 1 and 4, 2026 and, in November, notified regulators that 341,049 people had personal data, SSNs and health information potentially accessed.

## What happened

SecurityWeek reported, on November 17, 2026, the official notice from Access Dental Management (ADM), a Texas-based dental services provider. The company said it discovered the incident on May 5 and launched an investigation with forensic specialists. The investigation found that, between May 1 and 4, attackers accessed the network and viewed or copied certain data, including personal information.

On October 21, the review of the compromised information determined that the attacker accessed names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, medical information and health plan data. In the week before the report, ADM notified the attorneys general of Texas and Vermont that 341,049 people were affected (341,039 in Texas and 10 in Vermont) and began sending letters to potentially affected individuals, advising them to monitor statements, explanations of benefits and credit reports. The company operates clinics in Texas, Illinois, Indiana and New Mexico. The official notice did not detail the attack vector.

## Why it matters

There is a long gap between the access (early May), the end of the scoping (October 21) and the public notification to regulators (November). SSNs and health data are identifiers and records that the individual cannot replace as easily as a password. Without a map available earlier, those affected only start watching their own history when the letter arrives.
