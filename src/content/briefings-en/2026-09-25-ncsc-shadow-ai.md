**In one line:** the NCSC says shadow AI does not go away with a ban; it goes away with visibility, an approved alternative and control over what the agent can reach.

## What happened

On 7 September 2026 the National Cyber Security Centre published the blog The hidden risks of shadow AI. Shadow AI is the use of AI outside approved systems and processes. The agency cites a Microsoft survey in the UK according to which 71% of employees have already used an unapproved tool. Named risks: incident, loss of IP, regulatory failure and loss of control when corporate data goes to a consumer service. Agents with loose guardrails inherit data, service and privilege; the attacker who exploits them inherits the same.

## Why it matters

For LATAM operations the message is governance, not a British product. LGPD and internal policies require a processing map. If the team pastes a contract or a CPF into a personal chatbot, the legal basis and the processing record do not follow the fact. DSPM shows where sensitive data lives; AISPM shows which AIs and agents reach it.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |

## What to do this week

1. Inventory AIs and agents in use — security + business units.
2. Separate data that may go to an approved model from data that must not leave.
3. Signal it worked: a named list, owner and destination for each tool, with no personal account on the critical flow.
