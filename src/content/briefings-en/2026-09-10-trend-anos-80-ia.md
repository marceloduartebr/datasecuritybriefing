In one line: the trend is not only a prompt — it is an upload of a recognisable face to a system whose inventory the company almost never sees.

## What happened

On Tuesday (8 Sep), O Tempo and O Globo described the “how I would look in the 1980s” chain on Instagram and TikTok. Google recorded a 5,000% rise in searches for “trend anos 80” that week. In stories alone, more than 5 million photos accompanied the sticker; O Globo cited at least 21 thousand posts with #trendanos80. The public flow is an upload of a sharp photo to ChatGPT or Gemini, with an explicit instruction to preserve the face.

## Why it matters

The product cut teaches the prompt. The governance angle is another: the identifiable photo leaves the personal account — often from a corporate phone — and enters a consumer model without the record-by-record map the LGPD requires to inform the data subject. A “do not send personal data to AI” policy does not appear in the same place where the sticker asks “add yours”.

A photo is not, by itself, biometric data. A facial template is. Treating the two as synonyms hides the real surface: identifiable personal data at a third party, with a child and an employee in the same flow.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Upload of a recognisable face to a consumer LLM | Public prompt asks to preserve features; 5 million stories | High |
| Retention / training at the vendor | Personal account, opaque deadline and legal basis for the data subject | Medium |
| Downstream and children (Digital ECA) | Sticker “add yours”; ANPD 2027-2028 agenda cites child, adolescent and AI | High |

## What to do this week

1. DPO / privacy — include “photo in consumer generative AI” in the ROPA and in the staff notice, separate from biometric data.
2. Security / IT — check whether the tenant blocks image upload to ChatGPT/Gemini on the managed device.
3. Signal it worked — one question answered: how many team selfies left the network this week, and on which legal basis.
