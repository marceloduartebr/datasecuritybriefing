In one line: a school going down is not the same as knowing which pupil record left.

## What happened

On 25 August 2026 Kaspersky published a cybersecurity snapshot of education: from January 2025 to June 2026, ransomware accounted for 60% of cyberattacks on private schools and universities in Brazil. São Paulo concentrated 60% of the incidents in the survey; Rio de Janeiro and Pernambuco, 20% each. Most cited vectors: a legitimate account with a reused or shared password, an outdated system exposed to the internet, improper internal access and poorly used remote-access software. Initial containment in minutes or a few hours; investigation, forensics and recovery, on average, 9.6 hours. In the firm’s cut, the public network appears more in privilege escalation and internal access; the private one, in financially motivated ransomware.

## Why it matters

The percentage hides the data type. A school stores CPF, address and contact of the pupil and the guardian. When that leaks, the next step is rarely only a cancelled class: it is tuition phishing and SIM swap. Public and private suffer different dynamics; the gap is the same — a map of where the child’s record sits and who still has access after a staff account falls.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Ransomware in the private sector | 60% of attacks in the Kaspersky cut | High |
| Legitimate account / reused password | Same report | High |
| Child data in the school record | Operational inference from the type of base | High |

## What to do this week

1. List systems with pupil and guardian records and who has an active account — owner: IT + school office.
2. Treat a compromised staff account as a child-data incident, not only an availability issue.
3. Signal it worked: within hours, which pupil bases that access reached.
