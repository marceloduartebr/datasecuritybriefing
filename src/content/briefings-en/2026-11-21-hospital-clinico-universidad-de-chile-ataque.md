**In one line:** containing the attack and knowing what was exposed are different problems; the second depends on an inventory made beforehand.

## What happened
The Hospital Clínico of the Universidad de Chile confirmed a cyberattack that began on 14 November 2026, detected by its IT team, against institutional servers and digital platforms. The official website went offline and appointment booking via the web and the call center was interrupted. The hospital says clinical and care continuity remains in operation and that it is coordinating the response with the Agencia Nacional de Ciberseguridad (ANCI) and the university. According to BioBioChile (17 November), there are doubts about the scope and about whether patient data was affected. Members of Congress sent official letters citing Law 20.584 (confidentiality of medical records) and Law 21.663 (Cybersecurity Framework Law, which requires reporting of significant incidents).

## Why it matters
Health data is permanent: there is no way to "change" an exposed diagnosis. The reporting obligation in Law 21.663 and the confidentiality of medical records require the hospital to know, quickly, which records were on the affected systems.

## What to do this week
1. List where clinical data lives outside the main system (exports, spreadsheets, support servers) — owner: DPO and IT.
2. Define the significant-incident reporting procedure with deadlines and owners — owner: information security and legal.
3. Sign that it worked: for any server, the team can say within hours which categories of sensitive data it holds.
