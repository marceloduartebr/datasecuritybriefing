In one line: TfL confirmed that an incident on the Oyster photocard website, detected on November 16, may have exposed data on about 265,000 customers, including the security question and answer, but not passwords, payments or children's data.

## What happened

Transport for London said that on November 16, 2026 it detected suspicious activity on the Oyster photocard website after customers began receiving emails asking them to create an account they already had. According to the organization, the emails came from TfL's own systems as a result of the compromise. The site was taken offline. TfL said it had contained the incident, notified the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) and contacted those affected.

Chief technology and revenue strategy officer Shashi Verma told the BBC that about 265,000 photocard customers may have had account data compromised. Name, email address, phone number, home address, date of birth and the security question and answer were cited. TfL's current assessment is that photos, passwords, bank or card details, travel information, and the names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster were not compromised. Verma said no children's data was compromised.

Account types that may have been affected include Zip Oyster (ages 5-10, 11-15 and 16+), 18+ Student, 18-25 Care Leaver, 60+ London, Apprentice and Veterans. The cards remain valid for travel. The BBC noted it is the second time in just over two years that the photocard system has gone offline because of a cyber incident.

## Why it matters

TfL says passwords and payment data did not leave, but the security question and answer did. That pair is often used to reset access on other services. The public figure already exists (about 265,000) and the investigation continues. An inventory that does not treat the security question as authentication data underestimates the reach of the incident.

## What to do this week

1. Privacy and security: if employees, travelers or dependants hold an Oyster photocard, log the incident and the official scope (including the security question) in the exposed-data inventory. Owner: DPO / information security.
2. Identity and access: review where the company still uses security questions and answers for account recovery and plan to replace that factor. Owner: identity and access.
3. Customer service: advise those notified to ignore the "create account" email, change their security question and reused passwords, and treat only TfL's channels as official. Owner: customer service / fraud prevention.
