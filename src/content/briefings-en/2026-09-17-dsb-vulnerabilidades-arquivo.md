In one line: the leak is the last chapter; the archive now keeps a desk for the hole that can still be closed.

## What happened

Data Security Briefing gained the Vulnerabilities category. The archive at dsb.duarte.top no longer has only two desks (Incident and News) and now stores, in the same cut, KEV, CPU, EPSS and the software the region actually runs — perimeter, MSP, edge, supply chain, identity, AI and VoIP.

The About page describes the goal: a single reading point for whoever decides on data in Latin America.

## Why it matters

Whoever reads only the incident arrives after the data has left. A written policy does not replace an inventory of edge, identity and third parties. The new desk is not a CVE newsletter: it is the same evidence-versus-policy angle, with what to do this week.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Incident-only archive | The case enters when it is already a crisis | High |
| CVE without an internal owner | KEV/CPU with no asset map | Medium |
| Third party with more access than the team | MSP, VoIP, supply chain on the KEV | High |

## What to do this week

1. Check whether the risk team reads Vulnerabilities in the same place it reads the leak — owner: CISO / governance.
2. Cross the week’s cut (perimeter, MSP, edge, identity) with the real inventory — owner: operations.
3. Signal it worked: a documented patch or access restriction before the next statement.
