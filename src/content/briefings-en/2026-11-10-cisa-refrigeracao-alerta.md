In one line: the same forgotten asset that causes breaches now puts stock and equipment at physical risk.

## What happened
On 5 November 2026, CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the US Department of Transportation published a joint technical advisory on ongoing malicious activity against industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems, used in health, chemical, food and agriculture and transport sectors. According to the alert, actors exploit electronic temperature and commercial refrigeration controllers that are unpatched or internet-accessible, in supermarkets and cold warehouses, and can alter the temperature, with risk of fire, stock loss and reduced equipment lifespan. Recommendations include taking the devices off the internet, changing default passwords, applying patches and replacing equipment without manufacturer support. According to specialised press, the alert follows waves of attacks on supermarkets and a blood bank in France.

## Why it matters
Operational controllers are usually installed and maintained by vendors, with remote access and factory passwords, outside the IT inventory. It is the same condition behind many breaches: a connected asset that nobody knows exists and whose access nobody reviews.

## Risk reading
| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Controllers internet-accessible or unpatched | Joint advisory of 5 November | High |
| Default passwords and unsupported equipment | Alert recommendations | High |
| Remote access by maintenance vendors | Cases reported in Europe | Medium |

## What to do this week
1. Look for temperature and refrigeration controllers accessible from the internet and take them offline — owner: security and operations.
2. Change default passwords and review remote access of the vendors that maintain those devices — owner: facilities with security.
3. Signal that it worked: connected operational equipment appears in the same inventory as servers, with owner and responsible vendor.
